Scene+ Rewards: launching a loyalty platform at scale

Senior Digital Product Manager · Scotiabank Digital Factory · 2021

Scene+ is the evolution of the original SCENE program, Cineplex's loyalty program where members could redeem points for movie tickets. Scene+ expanded earning and redemption options with numerous partners in travel and merchandise.

I was responsible for Scotiabank's piece of Scene+. On the business side, that meant a heavy lift of coordination across business, regulatory, legal, marketing, and external partners. On the technical side, it meant a full migration of the old program into the new system, plus new functionality: a redemption journey and marketing offers. And we had a can't-miss deadline, with national attention on the program.

The redemption journey was the trickiest part. We used single sign-on to bring users from the Scotiabank app into the Scene+ app seamlessly, and I got into the weeds of the engineering architecture, including the OAuth2 protocols. That's where I uncovered a flaw in the technical design: it called for back-end API calls between the two apps, but those calls couldn't be made silently, so users would have watched the apps toggle back and forth several times. Because we caught it early, we could put a workaround in place: we minimized the number of calls and added a disclaimer about the toggling. That preserved the experience and kept us on time for national launch.

Foreign Exchange Pricing Platform: zero to one on live rates

Product Owner (Contract) · Scotiabank · 2018 – 2021

The FX pricing platform takes the live interbank FX rate and translates it into a customer price for almost all Scotiabank channels, including retail, small business, wire payments, and ATMs.

This one started as a blank page. When I took over the platform, many channels were priced with a static rate that was only updated once a day. That was problematic because the rate had a lot of extra margin built in to protect against market movement, which made it less competitive for customers.

I took the platform from nothing to a live MVP in six months, then to the full vision within about a year. I did the database schema myself, all the wireframing and hi-fidelity UI once the MVP was out, and ran the internal go-to-market, mostly because there wasn't anyone else lined up to do it.

One particularly interesting challenge was standing up the MVP in such a short timeframe. It took some out-of-the-box thinking and an aggressively minimal scope: I realized it was viable to launch with no user interface at all and manage the system with command-line functions. It was a tough sell to just about everyone involved, but it got us out the door and gave us breathing room to build the UI later. It's still pricing FX across every Scotiabank retail channel today.

Mobile App Platform: sizing for retention, not just for size

Platform Product Lead (Contract) · lululemon · 2025 – 2026

The app had been quietly growing for years, and the thing that worried me wasn't disk space in the abstract, it was retention. People delete large apps to free up room well before they actually run out of it, so a ballooning install size is a churn risk wearing an engineering metric's clothes.

The harder question was what target actually mattered. "As small as possible" isn't a plan, so I went and looked at how people actually run into app size on iOS: open iPhone Storage settings and apps are listed largest first, but only the top 20 show, the rest hide behind a "Show More" button. Most people never see an app flagged as a space hog unless it's big enough to crack that list. So the target became staying under that fold, not chasing an arbitrary minimum.

To turn that into an actual number, I commissioned a statistical analysis, with some LLM help, on installed-app-size distributions: what size would a typical device's apps need to clear to land in that visible top 20 (roughly the 95th percentile, two standard deviations out). The answer came back around 440MB, so that became the number everyone argued about in planning meetings for the next while.

Scheduled Interac eTransfer: building for recurring, not one-off, transfers

Senior Digital Product Manager · Scotiabank Digital Factory · 2021 – 2024

I owned product for Interac e-Transfer and Add Bill Payee across Scotiabank's whole retail base. The team's early instinct, understandably, was to optimize the single transaction: fewer taps, faster confirmation, a higher completion rate on any one transfer or payment.

I pushed back on that framing. A single successful transfer doesn't make a customer sticky, a recurring one does. Once you've set up a recurring e-Transfer or a scheduled bill payment through your bank, switching banks means rebuilding all of that somewhere else, which is real friction, and it's friction working in the bank's favour. So we shifted priority from "make one transfer easier" to "make setting up a recurring transfer at least as easy as a one-off," on the bet that recurring usage, not raw transaction count, is what actually keeps people around.

App Review Intelligence Tool

Problem Space

At every organization I've worked at, there's been some form of customer success team whose job includes reviewing customer feedback and passing suggestions to Product. The trouble was that they often collected and delivered those suggestions in a silo, and the meetings between the teams frequently ended up unproductive.

Opportunity

As a prototype, I built a Python tool that scrapes App Store and Google Play reviews by app and date range, then runs LLM-powered analysis to surface themes, sentiment, and prioritized product recommendations. It's a faster alternative to manual feedback synthesis, and it puts that analysis directly in Product's hands.

The tool could extend well beyond App Store reviews, and it likely makes sense to build it into a Claude skill or an equivalent, depending on which LLM an organization uses. The prototype is on GitHub (linked above).

LLM-Powered Knowledge Management System

Problem Space

In fast-paced environments, or even just day-to-day life, it's hard to hold every important detail in working memory, and context-switching is exhausting.

On top of that, as LLM tools become more common in personal and working life, chatbots have a key limitation: a limited context window.

Opportunity

I built an LLM-powered personal knowledge base based on Andrej Karpathy's LLM wiki idea: a structured, sourced wiki spanning career, financial, and life decisions, built on a disciplined schema for source provenance and contradiction tracking.

What makes my version more distinctive is that I forked it into a specialized version for collaboration. The first use case was a family business, where collaborators could organize all their documents and meetings in one place, and it helped guide a real governance decision.

I'd be excited to apply the concept next to a product management team, where the system could help organize information, facilitate communication, and ultimately navigate the often stressful challenges of the PM space.

No public demo is available, since it contains sensitive information, but reach out to me to find out more.
